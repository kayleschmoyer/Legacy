Draft board · small viewport study

Five small-screen boards that name names

All five replace the position-glyph grid with the player. Same 12-team snake, same data as the desktop board — the difference is which axis survives the narrow screen: time, team, the board itself, or the round. Shown at 375 px.

A

Pick feed

Time is the axis. Newest round first, one row per pick: name as headline, position folded into the club-and-career line, signature stat on the right, and a chip when the pick beat consensus. The old position glyph column is gone — it was saying what the color edge already says.

B

Team pager

Team is the axis. Swipe or tap through the twelve rosters; each shows what that team actually has, slot by slot, with open slots called out and a net-value line at the bottom. Best for the question a phone user actually asks mid-draft: what does he have, and what does he still need?

C

Pocket grid

The board survives intact. Real columns and rounds, just 104 px wide — enough for the name, the club, and the stat — with the team header pinned on top and drag-scroll left/right. Keeps the graphical read of the desktop board instead of substituting a list for it.

D

Round runner

Round is the axis. Numbered round tabs like a draft-tracker, then the round itself drawn as the snake it is: one continuous track, a colored node per pick, gold node for you, and the turn called out at the wrap. A stacked bar above shows what the round was made of — the round gets a shape you can remember.

E

Round tiles

The round as one screenful. Twelve teams, three across, four down, at the same cell height as the pocket grid — the whole round with no scrolling. Round header carries the position mix and the snake direction; your squad slider stays pinned at the bottom, and the gold button raises the available-player filter so you can draft without leaving the board.
