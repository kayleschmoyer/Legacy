Six football directions

Agreed — the ticket is baseball's object: paper, sepia, "admit one," a day at the ballpark. Football's hero should come from football's own visual world. Each of these fills the exact same slots as the ticket hero, so swapping is a component change, not a page change. Reference content throughout: Steel Curtain Era, Week 9, live matchup. Direction E works from the 1990s broadcast scorebug reference.

Direction A

Scoreboard slab

The stadium board itself: bolted dark slab, a status strip carrying the football-only facts a ticket can't hold (quarter, clock, down & distance, possession), team blocks flanking a center rule, league identity and actions on the base. Strongest fit for a live in-progress matchup, and the numbers get real size.

Direction B

Broadcast bar

The TV score bug, blown up to hero scale: angled turf flag, two team cells with records, quarter and clock on the tail. It sits low and wide, so it takes far less vertical space than the ticket — the leagues list moves up the page. Most modern of the four, and the least "archive."

Direction C

Chalk playbook

A coach's board: dark slate-green, faint route diagonals, a dashed chalk frame, uppercase league name, chalk-white numerals with a gold rule under the leader. Closest in spirit to the ticket — an artifact from the sport, era-friendly — without borrowing baseball's paper. Also the calmest against the night-stadium page.

Direction D

Field position

Identity and score up top, and the hero's base is the field: end-zone caps for both teams, yard lines and hash marks across, and a gold marker showing where the matchup actually stands — here, drive position. The one direction that adds information rather than restyling it; also the one that needs a real data point to justify the marker, or it becomes decoration.

Direction E · from the 1990s reference

Broadcast scorebug

The period bug, at hero scale and not flattened: chrome bevel frame, stacked team plates with hard highlights, chiselled type, possession arrow, machined clock bar. It keeps football's own vocabulary and lands on era — which is the product's whole premise — where the modern broadcast bar (B) reads as any fantasy app. It also solves the sizing problem for free: the bug is a fixed 250px object, so it never has to reflow with the rail.

Direction F · C + E

Playbook board with a mounted bug

The chalkboard carries identity, era and actions; the bug runs the board's full height and carries the score, each team's record, and — in the machined bar where broadcast puts down & distance — the fantasy facts that actually matter: week and time remaining. Each does what it is good at — the board is era and place, the bug is standing and deadline — and the hero stops trying to make one surface do both. Practically: the bug is a fixed 250px object, so the reflow problem that dogged the ticket at lg disappears; the board just gets narrower.
